# Level 1 — Floor Scorecard (roving expert crews) · v01 DRAFT

**Purpose:** prove the team found a real pain point and the agent moved it.
**Who:** 2 expert crews — Crew A on floor 1, Crew B on floor 5. Visit every team on
your floor.
**Output:** top 4 per floor advance (4+4 = 8 finalists). Scores banked for the five
pillar awards.

## Visit protocol

- Crews circulate **from 5 PM**. Early laps are *formative* (advice, unblocking,
  mentor-style — no scores). The **scoring lap (~7:30 PM)** scores.
- Fixed format per scoring visit (~10 min) — **five steps, in order**. Start at the
  pain; only then the details.
- Judges score independently on the card before walking to the next table; no
  discussing scores between tables (anchoring).
- **Rank within your floor only. Never compare across floors.** Top 4 per floor
  advance.
- Progression counts: a team that iterated hard since kickoff is showing exactly
  the behavior this event rewards.
- Teams are identified by the printed **name plate** on each table.

## Qualifying bar (pass/fail — see README)

☐ Genuinely agentic ☐ Ran on the real project ☐ Pillar declared

A team missing a check is still scored but flagged; a flagged team cannot advance.

## The five steps (0–5 each · total /25)

The 0–5 scale reads the same on every step: **0–1 a claim** (told, not shown) ·
**2–3 a demonstration** (shown on the real project, roughly) · **4–5 a measurement**
(proven, repeatable, honest about limits).

### Step 1 — The Pain
*Is this a pain point worth elevating?*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Invented for the demo, or trivial — nobody would pay to remove it |
| 2–3 | Real but peripheral; the team can name who feels it |
| 4–5 | Real, recurring, costly — the project's actual bottleneck; team can say what it costs today |

### Step 2 — The Mapping
*Pain → declared pillar → measure: does the chain hold?*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Pain and declared pillar don't match; no measure named |
| 2–3 | Right pillar, fuzzy measure |
| 4–5 | Clean chain with a defined before/after measure — a real elevation path |

Teams declaring 2 pillars are scored on the **stronger** chain — declaring wide
splits your evidence; it never doubles your score.

### Step 3 — The Agent
*What role does the human play?* (Feng et al. 2025, DeepMind Levels of AGI, HF smolagents.)

| Score | Anchor — the human is… |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | …the operator: prompt chain / scripted workflow; human drives every step |
| 2–3 | …steering: agent picks tools / runs multi-step work but needs babysitting |
| 4–5 | …the approver: agent owns the stage end-to-end; 5 = autonomy deliberately matched to the stage's risk |

Note: 5 is *not* "no human." Observer-only autonomy caps at 4 unless the stage's
risk genuinely permits it.

### Step 4 — The Gate
*Where does the human decide — show it firing.* (Anthropic safe-agents, LangChain HITL.)

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | No gate, or a confirm-button the team can't explain |
| 2–3 | Gate at a sensible point; human sees what the agent proposes |
| 4–5 | Shows **why** (evidence, diff) · reject/edit incorporated · at the consequential action |

### Step 5 — The Proof
*Did the declared pillar actually move?*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Slideware, mock data, or movement merely asserted |
| 2–3 | Rough run on the real project; movement plausible, warts visible |
| 4–5 | Measured movement on the declared pillar; repeated runs, candid on failures & cost |

## Why five equal steps (design note)

Pain (1) + Mapping (2) + Proof (5) = 15 of 25 points — **effectiveness is
structurally the majority of the score**, which encodes "effectiveness over
complexity" without a weighting multiplier. The Agent and The Gate carry the
event's agentic thesis at 10 of 25.
