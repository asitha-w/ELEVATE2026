Elevate 2026 · Anchors — what teams elevate & how it's proven

Five surfaces. One elevation path each.

Teams declare 1–2 surfaces at registration. Judging starts at the pain: is it real, is it mapped to the right surface, and did that surface move?
Higher Pace
ship faster, every bolt
The pain looks like

Long lead times, waiting on hand-offs, "that takes a sprint"

The elevation path

Agent owns the slow step end-to-end; humans decide at the gate

Proof that counts

Time a real task before vs. with the agent — lead time / throughput delta

Higher Quality
gated, tested, reliable
The pain looks like

Rework, escaped defects, flaky releases, "almost right" output

The elevation path

Agent tests, reviews or gates every change before it lands

Proof that counts

Rework / change-failure rate down; defects caught at the gate, shown

Greater Value
outcomes clients feel
The pain looks like

Effort burns on toil while the customer's KPI stands still

The elevation path

Agent absorbs the toil; effort shifts to what the customer pays for

Proof that counts

A named customer KPI, before/after; % effort moved from toil to new capability

Stronger Partnership
handholding through change
The pain looks like

Slow feedback loops, opaque progress, surprises at the demo

The elevation path

Agent keeps the customer in the loop with evidence, continuously

Proof that counts

A customer-visible artifact the customer validated; feedback loop measurably faster

Developer Happiness
engaged, fulfilled teams
The pain looks like

Toil, cognitive overload, dread work nobody wants to own

The elevation path

Agent takes the toil; humans keep the judgment and the interesting work

Proof that counts

Before/after team survey + time reclaimed; "would you keep it on Monday?"
