# The Floor Scorecard (single round, roving pairs) · v02 DRAFT

**Purpose:** prove the team found a real pain, the agent moved it — **and it's
worth something beyond the demo.**
**Who:** ten judges in five pairs, one floor, each pair owning a fixed route of
~6 stations.
**Output:** each pair shortlists 2 → 10 candidates → the full panel deliberates
over dinner → **Judges' Pick 8**, announced 8:15. The eight then teach the floor;
nothing after this card is scored.

## Visit protocol

- Pairs circulate **from 5 PM**. Early laps are *formative* (advice, unblocking,
  mentor-style — no scores). The **scoring lap runs 7:00–8:00 PM**.
- Fixed format per scoring visit (~10 min) — **six steps, in order**. Start at the
  pain; end at the offering.
- Judges score independently on the card before walking to the next table; no
  discussing scores between tables (anchoring).
- **Rank within your route only. Never compare across routes.** Each pair names its
  strongest 2; cross-route reconciliation happens only in the full-panel
  deliberation, with cards on the table.
- Progression counts: a team that iterated hard since kickoff is showing exactly
  the behavior this event rewards.
- Teams are identified by the printed **name plate** on each table.

## Qualifying bar (pass/fail — see README)

☐ Genuinely agentic ☐ Ran on the real project ☐ Pillar declared

A team missing a check is still scored but flagged; a flagged team cannot be picked.

## The six steps (0–5 each · total /30)

The 0–5 scale reads the same on every step: **0–1 a claim** (told, not shown) ·
**2–3 a demonstration** (shown on the real project, roughly) · **4–5 a measurement**
(proven, repeatable, honest about limits).

### Step 1 — The Pain
*Is this a pain point worth elevating?*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Invented for the demo, or trivial — nobody would pay to remove it |
| 2–3 | Real but peripheral; the team can name who feels it |
| 4–5 | Real, recurring, costly — the project's actual bottleneck; team can say what it costs today |

### Step 2 — The Mapping
*Pain → declared pillar → measure: does the chain hold?*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Pain and declared pillar don't match; no measure named |
| 2–3 | Right pillar, fuzzy measure |
| 4–5 | Clean chain with a defined before/after measure — a real elevation path |

Teams declaring 2 pillars are scored on the **stronger** chain — declaring wide
splits your evidence; it never doubles your score.

### Step 3 — The Agent
*What role does the human play?* (Feng et al. 2025, DeepMind Levels of AGI, HF smolagents.)

| Score | Anchor — the human is… |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | …the operator: prompt chain / scripted workflow; human drives every step |
| 2–3 | …steering: agent picks tools / runs multi-step work but needs babysitting |
| 4–5 | …the approver: agent owns the stage end-to-end; 5 = autonomy deliberately matched to the stage's risk |

Note: 5 is *not* "no human." Observer-only autonomy caps at 4 unless the stage's
risk genuinely permits it.

### Step 4 — The Gate
*Where does the human decide — show it firing.* (Anthropic safe-agents, LangChain HITL.)

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | No gate, or a confirm-button the team can't explain |
| 2–3 | Gate at a sensible point; human sees what the agent proposes |
| 4–5 | Shows **why** (evidence, diff) · reject/edit incorporated · at the consequential action |

**Probe it (the stress-test question, folded in from the old pitch round):** ask
for the case the agent gets wrong. "It can't break" is a red flag; "it breaks at
X, the blast radius is Y, the gate contains it because Z" is a top answer — even
if X is unhandled.

### Step 5 — The Proof
*Did the declared pillar actually move?*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Slideware, mock data, or movement merely asserted |
| 2–3 | Rough run on the real project; movement plausible, warts visible |
| 4–5 | Measured movement on the declared pillar; repeated runs, candid on failures & cost |

Testimony is a claim: in the METR RCT, developers *believed* +20% while measuring
−19%. The proof ladder applies — **measured > projected > claimed**; a projected
number with a real baseline and visible math scores as a demonstration.

### Step 6 — The Offering *(new in v02 — the Extended Value step)*
*Is this a real offering — new upside that AI-DLC unlocks, beyond this one demo?*

**What it measures:** every team has a customer by construction — that scores
nothing. This step measures the distance to "the customer would run this on
Monday": (a) a **named user** whose Monday changes; (b) the **workflow it lands
in** — embedded in how people already work; (c) **unit economics** — what one run
costs vs. what one run is worth; (d) the **honest gap list** — what's missing
before 99x can sell this; (e) **repeatability** — another 99x team could adopt
the pattern on a different project.

**The Q&A test:** "if the customer says yes tomorrow, what must you build first,
and what does a month of running it cost?" — and "what did you stop doing?"

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | A demo with no named user, no workflow, no cost story |
| 2–3 | Named user & workflow; rough cost-to-run; honest gap list |
| 4–5 | Named user + workflow + unit economics + transferable pattern — 99x could scope the offering from this visit |

## Why six equal steps (design note)

Pain (1) + Mapping (2) + Proof (5) + Offering (6) = 20 of 30 points —
**effectiveness and value are structurally the majority of the score**, encoding
both "effectiveness over complexity" and the North Star's Extended Value pillar
without a weighting multiplier. The Agent and The Gate carry the event's agentic
thesis at 10 of 30.

## Selection mechanics

1. Each pair sums its cards → ranks its ~6 stations → **shortlists 2** (flagged
   teams excluded).
2. Over dinner the full panel of ten walks the 10 candidates: confirm the
   qualifying bar, compare cards, settle the **Judges' Pick 8**. Ties break on
   **The Gate** (the event's trust thesis), then panel vote.
3. Every team — picked or not — receives its **2–3 written notes** at the 8:15
   announcement: concrete and fixable ("value claim has no number", "we never saw
   the gate fire").
4. Separately, the panel lists which entries it recommends to the **99x expert
   panel → customer as a real offering** — the list need not equal the eight.

## Card layout

Single A4 landscape card per team per judge — six step rows + qualifying-bar
